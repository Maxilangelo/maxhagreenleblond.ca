Welcome / Mount Finlayson

Welcome to my website! Here you can find bits of my writing on travelling, music, and other epic things. You can find some photos that I took that I think are pretty cool. And soon I’m going to make a section for music that I’ve made. I’m so excited to finally have this at a stage where I feel like I can share it with people so yeah, please enjoy. I’m working on writing about my Japan trip I went on with my bud Emrys last February and also writing about my epic Europe trip this summer. Those blog posts are going to be truly holy scriptures so please come back and read them when I’m done.

For now, maybe I’ll share a story from recently. A few days ago I went on a hike up Mount Finlayson with my friend Oliver. It was pouring rain as I drove out with my dog Ruby to pick him up. When we got out to Goldstream, the parking lot was packed because there were lots of people trying to see the salmon run. Thankfully we got some rockstar parking and headed out for a nice hike. As we passed the river, we saw a few ragged looking salmon trying to make their way upstream. They really do look quite ugly by that point in their journey, and the smell emanating from the river is not a good one.

The start of Mount Finlayson is pretty steep and muddy, especially since for some reason you can’t take the stairs anymore (maybe there was a landslide?). Oliver, in his running shoes, somehow managed not to slip and fall as we were making our way up the muddy trail. Ruby ran ahead, chewing on branches and drinking from streams. As we walked, me and Oliver caught up on what we’ve been up to recently. The rain had stopped but the air was wet.

Eventually the trail opened up to this super steep rocky section. We made our way up, passing a few hikers and saying hello. I love how friendly people are when you’re out hiking, it's such a good vibe. The trail got steeper and steeper, and we started having a bit of a hard time finding the right route up. I started getting a bit worried about Ruby since there were some real sharp drop offs on the right of the trail, and of course my dog has to aurafarm and stand right on the edge of each cliff. So I put her on the leash and we continued on our merry way.

This is actually at PKOLS, I didn't take any photos of the Finlayson hike. But you get the idea, she likes to aurafarm.

Finally the path got less steep and I was able to let Ruby run ahead. We found a nice spot to sit and enjoy the view with some snacks that I brought. It’s pretty cool seeing Victoria from that angle. It was actually my first time going up all the way to the peak and I gotta say the view is worth the cardio for sure. We munched our apples then headed back down the path.

Once we got back to the steep section I put Ruby back on the leash. But now there was an issue, going down the hill Ruby started pulling on the leash. We got to a really steep section and I had to let go of her so she could jump down it. As I was going down myself, she went on ahead. I told her to wait but she kept walking closer to the edge of the cliff. When she got there, she decided now was a good time to shake herself off (you know how dogs do when they’re wet). Oh my god, in that moment I was certain she was gonna stumble just the slightest bit and send herself off the cliff. For the rest of the way down I kept her tight on the leash, but she kept on pulling, maybe because she was stressed out, or maybe she just wanted to go up ahead. Multiple times she almost pulled me or Oliver to our deaths along with her. One time, as I was making my way down this long, slick slab of rock, she pulled just the slightest bit and I slipped backwards and tumbled down for a good ten meters, hitting myself on all sorts of rocks along the way. Thankfully my camera and my phone which were in the backpack were fine.

Once we made it past that steep section, I took Ruby off the leash, thankful to not have to deal with her bullshit. On the way down we talked about good movies that we’d seen recently and movies with good soundtracks. When we got back to the parking lot, I realized that I had left the leash back at the bottom of that steep section. In my eagerness to get her off the leash, I must’ve left it right there on the trail.

So yup, that’s my story. I hope it was readable and maybe at best, somewhat enjoyable.

Thanks for reading and have a great day!
